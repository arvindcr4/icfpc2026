So the whole remaining problem is speed. Where to look:

  * The grid is 4,218 x 826 (3.5 MB). Cost per interpreted step tends to scale with how much of
    the layout a man must traverse, so a very tall grid can mean thousands of wasted ticks per
    operation. Shortening the walked paths matters more than shrinking total area.
  * Avoid work proportional to the whole board where a partial scan suffices — e.g. a full ring
    rotation per cell lookup inside a loop that already runs once per cell is quadratic.
  * The shortest path to each flag is at most 64 moves and the board is 16x16 = 256 cells, so a
    BFS should be a few thousand elementary operations, not millions. If your step count is far
    above that, the overhead is in the interpreter plumbing rather than the algorithm.
  * Consider whether frames are being recomputed from scratch when an incremental update would do.

For calibration: accepted programs on this contest average about 1.5 million ticks per case, and
one accepted program is 9.4 MB, so neither ticks nor area is scarce in absolute terms — but you
are evidently well past the per-case step budget, so the cost per interpreted step is the thing
to attack.
